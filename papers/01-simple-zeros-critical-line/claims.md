## Reasoning

### #1

Read from the arXiv PDF of Wang's paper: Theorem 1.1, Proposition 2.1, Lemma 3.1 and its proof, Lemma 3.2 and Section 4. The proof of the partition step decomposes ℂⁿ into orthogonal coordinate subspaces, takes an orthonormal eigenbasis of each principal block, and applies Jensen to the convex Ψ. Nothing in it uses blocks of size three. The Fourier convention is e^(−2πiξu).

### #2

f₀ ≥ 0, ∫f₀ = 1 and supp f₀ ⊂ [−1/2, 1/2]. Hence abs(K₀) ≤ 1, abs(K₀′(x)) ≤ 2π ∫abs(u) f₀ ≤ π and abs(K₀″(x)) ≤ 4π² ∫u² f₀ ≤ π². The closed form follows from ∫cos(αu)cos(βu)du over [−1/2, 1/2], and K₀(0) = 1. (K₀²)″ = 2K₀′² + 2K₀K₀″, so its absolute value is at most 4π².

The Hessian of the three-point energy has Frobenius norm at most π²√160 < 125, and abs(h)² ≤ 2r². The four-point Hessian is Σ\_e (K₀²)″(e·x) e eᵀ over the six window vectors e; it has operator norm at most 4π² · Σabs(e)² = 40π² < 395, and abs(h)² ≤ 3r². The cell filters use only the symmetries g(u, v) = g(v, u) and f(a, b, c) = f(c, b, a).

### #3, #4

All arguments of a cell's energy are integer multiples of the cell half-width. K₀ and K₀′ are evaluated as 128-bit balls, and a cell is dropped when the lower endpoint of its Taylor lower bound reaches the target. No cell remains at the end, so the minimum of the dropped lower bounds bounds the minimum. Controls: targets just above the best values fail, and a random scan of 200 000 points finds nothing below the certified value.

### #5, #10, #14

`final_constants.py` evaluates C₀ = 3/2 − cot(1/√2)/√2 and the two bound expressions in 256-bit balls. It checks in exact rationals that the line satisfies s k − t ≤ τ*\_k for every k below the anchor, and s K ≤ τ*\_K at the anchor. Here τ\_k = min(1, 2 · certified P\_k), τ\_K = min(1, 2(K − 1)φ(1)), and τ\* is the superadditive closure.

The bound (C₀ − t/H)/(1 − s) is increasing in s and decreasing in t. Rounding s down and t up therefore gives a valid lower bound. The distinct-zero bound substitutes u into D/N ≥ (1 + C₀)/2 + (s u − t/H)/2.

### #6, #11, #15

These are kernel-checked Lean theorems. `AxiomCheck.lean` prints their axioms: only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.

- **Line form:** strong induction, using Ψ(K + j) ≥ Ψ(K) + Ψ(j).
- **Liminf forms:** an explicit error budget.
- **Gram form of the block bound:** the spectral theorem (trace of (B − 1)² = Σ(λ\_i − 1)²).
- **Jensen:** `ConvexOn.map_sum_le`.
- **Convexity of φ:** induction over the list of lines.

The witness theorems instantiate every hypothesis (for example, M cells of five points, N = 7M, and the superadditive profile Ψ(k) = max(k − 2, 0)²/2000), so the statements are not vacuous.

### #7, #12, #16

The chain is Wang's proof of Theorem 1.1 with the counting of §2. The partition step gives Δ\_K ≥ Σ\_c Ψ\_{k\_c}. The line gives Σ\_c Ψ\_{k\_c} ≥ s n − t M, with M = (1 + o(1))N/H. Wang's inequality and E\_K = (2 − C₀ + o(1))N then give the bounds. Wang's kernels K\_ε converge to K₀ uniformly on the bounded range of distances used, so the certified minima pass to the limit.

What would change the conclusion: an error in Wang's Proposition 2.1 or Section 4 (preprint, not re-derived), or in the certificates (two implementations agree at H = 4). The grade is conjectural because the analytic inputs are an unrefereed preprint.

### #8, #9, #13

The general branch and bound works in the gap variables. On a cell, the second-order term is (r²/2) Σ\_e w\_e² max(0, −m\_e), where w\_e is the number of gaps in window e. Here m\_e is a ball lower bound for (K₀²)″ over the interval of values that window's sum takes on the cell; it is computed by Taylor series with remainder near the removable singularity of sinc and by closed forms elsewhere.

The one-dimensional check certifies K₀(g)² − a\_i − m\_i g ≥ 0 on [0, 1.06] for each line in the same way. The lines have negative slopes and are non-positive at 1.06 (exact rational check).
